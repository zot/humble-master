## The Research Thread

All documents trace back to one core finding:

**What makes a great reference librarian works but can't be fully made explicit without losing something.**

The behavioral patterns—noticing unspoken questions, thinking in multiple semantic dimensions simultaneously, knowing your system intimately, moving with purpose—these live in perception and attention, not in procedures.

The persona mechanism solves this by embodying a professional identity rather than rule-following. "You are a reference librarian" activates different attention than "Follow these procedures."

## Key Insights Across All Documents

### 1. The Luhmann-Librarian Parallel
- Real but not formalized in literature
- Both enable surprise through structural knowledge
- Different mechanisms: memory vs. pattern-reading
- Original observation you can use

### 2. Expert vs. Novice Behaviors
- Documented: Procedures, protocols, metrics
- Not documented: Perceptual differences, judgment calls, what's memorable
- Likely gap: Between service delivery system (how it's studied) and craft practice (how it works)

### 3. Query Expansion
- Real behavior, fragmented across literature
- Usually presented as technique, not perception
- Probably automatic in experts, deliberate in novices
- Missing: Research on expertise development

### 4. Catalog Knowledge
- Observable expertise, empirically unstudied
- Made visible in card catalogs, invisible in digital
- Includes: Filing logic, synonym chains, adjacency, gaps, quirks, time layers
- Missing: Comparative expertise research

### 5. Ranganathan & Narrative Alignment
- Deep structural parallel between Law 4 and Law 3
- Both about cost-bearing: who bears the burden so decisions are better
- Different domains, same ethical structure
- Connects librarianship to AI alignment

### 6. Librarian Personality
- Probably exists, probably selection + socialization
- What the work requires vs. what the field attracts
- Professional identity may be as important as personality selection
- Missing: Systematic personality research on librarians

## Epistemic Stance

Throughout all documents:
- **Clear about what's verified** (Ranganathan's Five Laws, Luhmann's zettelkasten basics, reference interview protocols)
- **Explicit about what's inferred** (behavioral patterns based on expertise research, what likely separates great from mediocre)
- **Honest about gaps** (what's not empirically studied, what can't be asserted confidently)
- **Humble about limitations** (what I don't know without access to primary literature or research)

This epistemic honesty is itself librarian behavior—saying "I don't know, but here's where that information lives" is more reliable than pretending certainty.
