# Librarian & Zettelkasten Research Notes

## Research Status & Epistemic Humility

This document tracks what I've verified vs. what I'm inferring vs. what I don't know.

### 1. Luhmann Zettelkasten as Conversation Partner

#### Verified Facts
- Luhmann (1927-1998) German sociologist built 90,000-card system
- Used phrase "Gesprächspartner" (conversation partner)
- System used location-based addressing (1/2a3, etc.) not hierarchical
- High bidirectional cross-referencing enabled "surprising" connections
- Generated productivity: 58 books, hundreds of articles during lifetime

#### Primary Sources
- *How to Take Smart Notes* (Ahrens) — popularized the concept
- German scholarship on Luhmann's methods (need verification)

#### Key Uncertainty
- **"Surprising" mechanism:** Is surprise from "what you stored years ago" the same as reference librarian creating surprise through "knowledge of filing adjacencies"?
  - Luhmann: A→B→C connections you created, now following your own traces
  - Librarian: A→C connections they discovered in the *existing system's* structure
  - These might be fundamentally different, both labeled as "surprising"

#### Question Status
- **Is this parallel real?** Partially. Both involve:
  - Deferred retrieval (not immediately searching for something)
  - Structural knowledge enabling non-obvious connections
  - Serendipity through method
  
  But they work through different mechanisms:
  - Zettelkasten: You built the adjacency yourself; rediscovering it is memoir-like
  - Reference: The librarian reads *structure*, not personal history

- **Has anyone connected these?** Need to verify. The parallel feels like something someone *should* have noticed, but I can't confirm they have.

### 2. Behavioral Patterns of Great Reference Librarians

#### What's Documented
- Reference interview protocols (RUSA guidelines, formal training)
- Accuracy rates in retrieval studies
- User satisfaction metrics

#### What's Under-Documented (Tacit Knowledge)
- Perceptual/attentional shifts between expert and novice
- What "noticing" means in practice
- The judgment calls that don't appear in procedures

#### Institutional Knowledge
- Great librarians are *known* within their institutions (regular patrons remember them)
- This is cultural knowledge, not published knowledge
- Ethnographic study could access this; standard library science literature may not

#### Problem
- **Tacit-to-explicit gap:** Reference work is highly personalized. The moves that make someone "great" may be impossible to codify without distorting them.
- Example from other fields: Great teachers, great mechanics, great cooks all have this problem.

### 3. Query Expansion as Natural Librarian Behavior

#### Where It Appears in Literature
- Information Retrieval (computational): Synonym expansion, term-weighting, thesauri
- Controlled vocabularies: LCSH (Library of Congress Subject Headings), MeSH (medical)
- Controlled vocabulary theory: Broader terms (BT), Narrower terms (NT), Related terms (RT)

#### The Gap
- **Most literature treats query expansion as a system feature, not human behavior**
- Question: Do reference librarians *naturally* think "what else would this be filed under?" or do they apply a learned technique?

#### Hypothesis (to test)
- This might be named as part of "reference interview" process
- Or it might be embedded in "search strategy" pedagogy without a distinct name
- Or it might be so tacit that it's not formally named

#### Key Distinction
- **Explicit technique:** "Let me try these related terms"
- **Tacit practice:** Expert just *naturally* queries multiple vocabularies simultaneously

### 4. The Catalog as Instrument

#### Observable Difference
- Expert librarian at a catalog: Moves with purpose, knows implicit structure
- Novice patron at a catalog: Random browsing, sometimes lucky, mostly frustrated

#### What's Being Seen?
- Filing logic (alphabetical, hierarchical, by subject classification)
- Gaps in catalog (where the material *should* be but isn't)
- Adjacency knowledge (what's shelved near X)
- Synonym chains (what different times/systems called the same thing)

#### Analog vs. Digital Difference
- **Physical card catalog:** Browsable, spatial, tactile affordances. Librarian could literally point to adjacencies.
- **OPAC:** Searchable but not browsable (usually). Same knowledge required but expressed differently.

#### Study Gaps
- I don't know of empirical studies measuring "catalog knowledge" as an expertise dimension
- Would be measurable: time-to-target, number of queries needed, pattern of cross-referencing

### 5. Ranganathan's "Save the Reader's Time"

#### Basic Facts
- S. R. Ranganathan (1892-1972) Indian librarian
- Five Laws of Library Science (foundational text)
- Law 1: Books are for use
- Law 2: Every reader has a book
- Law 3: Every book has a reader
- Law 4: [Need to verify exact wording]
- Law 5: A library is a growing organism

#### Key Question
- **"Save the reader's time"** — is this Law 4, or is it a paraphrase of the general principle?
- **Depth of meaning:** Efficiency vs. something about bearing the cost
  - Efficiency reading: Fast access, fewer searches
  - Cost-bearing reading: *The librarian does the hard work so the reader doesn't have to*
  - These are different!

#### Status
- **Need primary source:** Five Laws actual text, Ranganathan's own interpretation
- The paraphrase might be modern reinterpretation

## Writing Angle (Tentative)

The honest version is: **Reference librarians and zettelkasten both work through structural knowledge, but they activate it differently.** The librarian *reads* existing structures; the zettelkasten user *rediscovers* their own choices. Both can surprise, but the surprise works through different mechanisms.

The deeper insight might be about **tacit knowledge in specialized domains.** What makes a great reference librarian is often invisible precisely because it's working knowledge, not declarative knowledge. Same with great zettelkasten builders — they internalize the system until the method becomes intuition.
