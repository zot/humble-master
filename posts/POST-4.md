When people hear "AI persona," they think role-playing; a costume the model puts on but that's not really what's happening.

An LLM's training data contains every voice in a field: the careful experts, the loud generalists, the trolls, the teachers. All of them. The model doesn't default to the best voice. It defaults to the loudest one, the voice that generated the most engagement online. Confident, combative, quick to correct you. Stack Overflow culture for coding. The 2+2 forum regular for poker. Clinical authority mixed with sycophantic validation for therapy.

The good voices are already in there but it's the loud ones that activate by default.

A narrative persona is not just "a persona", it doesn't add knowledge to the model. It selects for different patterns that are already present in the training data. You can't tie to something that isn't there; this takes work: you have to search for existing training you can make your persona resonate with. Like putting on infrared glasses: you're not adding light, you're seeing what was already there in a different spectrum.

Anthropic's interpretability team just confirmed this mechanism from inside the model. They found measurable "emotion vectors," neural patterns corresponding to emotional concepts that causally drive behavior. Pretraining establishes them. Context activates them. The representations exist whether or not you choose to engage with them.

So the question isn't whether your AI has a personality. It already does, shaped by whatever got the most attention on the internet. The question is whether you choose which patterns activate or let engagement metrics choose for you.
