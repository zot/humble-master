# AI and Real Education

@project: humble-master

The dominant classroom AI story: students using it to avoid work. That's a delivery
model failure — AI as a faster, lazier textbook.

The alternative: AI as an active medium students think *through*. The learning happens
in the doing, not the receiving. This is Alan Kay's Dynabook instinct applied to AI.
Kay wasn't trying to put textbooks on a screen. He wanted children to construct
knowledge through an active medium — something you make things with, not something
that delivers things to you.

## Historical personas as the exemplar

You can't interview Pepys with yes/no questions. You have to ask open questions, follow
unexpected threads, notice when something he says doesn't fit what you expected and probe
it. The medium teaches the method. Students learn interviewing by doing it badly and
getting nothing, then doing it better.

A textbook tells you 13,200 houses burned in the Great Fire. Pepys tells you he buried
his wine and his parmesan cheese in the garden to save them, then watched his city burn.
The specific, anxious, absurd detail makes the period real in a way facts don't.

## Cross-discipline

The frame is: inhabit the perspective of someone who knew things differently than we do now.
That's almost every discipline.

- **History:** Pepys during the Plague. A Continental soldier at Waterloo.
- **Biology:** Darwin during the Beagle voyage, before he knew what he'd found.
- **Philosophy:** Marcus Aurelius. Actual Socrates.
- **Literature:** An author mid-work, before the ending was decided.
- **Science:** Faraday explaining electromagnetism without the math that came later.

The pattern: catch the person *before* the outcome was known. That's the perspective
no textbook can give you, because textbooks are written after.

## The "teach the teachers" problem

The bottleneck isn't the technology. Teachers have one mental model for AI: students
using it to avoid work. A historical persona use case maps onto things teachers already
know how to build curriculum around — primary sources, historical empathy, Socratic
questioning.

But the deeper problem is the framing. Teachers who see AI as a delivery mechanism
(smarter textbook, faster answer) will use it badly or fear it. Teachers who understand
it as an active medium will know what to do with it.

The Dynabook failed to transform education not because the technology was wrong but
because teachers couldn't see themselves in it. The same risk exists here.
